B1 · /get-started

Onboarding

Does it explain the platform clearly?

Yes: a new learner knows what the platform is for, that the data is synthetic, and what happens next. Ready for demo

What works

  • Three-part promise (know your role, find where you stand, learn what helps) before any form.
  • A dedicated step says plainly that roles, questions and courses are synthetic and not connected to iGOT.
  • The draft notice is summarised in three points; the full text stays one click away.
  • Choosing a role previews required levels on a scale, so "Level 3 of 4" means something.

Still weak

  • No department or profile step (S-02 lists one; there is no API for it yet).
  • The notice text itself is still a legal draft.
  • Learners who already acknowledged the notice cannot see steps 1–2 again.

B2 · /assessment · /assessment/attempts/:id?q=N

Assessment

Does it feel like a real competency assessment rather than a basic questionnaire?

In form, yes. In content, not yet: the ten demo items are arithmetic, which is now the most visible gap. Structure ready, content thin

What works

  • An introduction states purpose, rules and competencies measured before anything starts.
  • One question at a time, a question grid with answered and current states, and a live save status.
  • Reloading keeps the question and saved answers; a failed save keeps the choice and offers a retry.
  • Submitting needs confirmation, which lists unanswered questions with links back to them.

Still weak

  • Questions are synthetic arithmetic, not statistical methodology or scenarios.
  • Only 10 items across 2 competencies; evidence stays "medium" at best.
  • No "mark for review" flag; leaving through the sidebar with a failed save is not blocked (the tab-close warning is).

Introduction

Status, facts, how it works, rules, and the role's competencies alongside.

Answering a question

Answered with Space on a focused option; the heading takes focus on Next.

Question grid after reload

Two answered, question 2 current, the rest outlined.

Submit confirmation

Escape closes it and returns focus to the button.

Defects the journeys caught and I fixed

  • Answering a question without ?q= in the URL jumped the learner to the next unanswered question.
  • Option letters showed the original answer-key letters in shuffled order (A, D, C, B).
  • axe flagged invalid <dl> markup in the onboarding facts (definition-list, dlitem).

B3 · /attempts/:id/result

Results

Does it tell the learner what to do next?

Yes, clearly, but both destinations are still the old dashboard, so the journey thins out after one click. Next steps land on legacy screens

What works

  • Score, then a one-sentence meaning ("Both competencies are below the level your role requires").
  • Each competency shows estimated vs required level, evidence strength and a plain explanation.
  • Three next steps name the competency to focus on and the actual suggested courses.
  • Answer review shows your answer, the correct one and why; the method sits in a closed disclosure.

Still weak

  • "View competency gaps" and "See recommended learning" open the legacy dashboard.
  • No course detail page, so a suggested course cannot be opened or started.
  • Reassessment is out of scope, so a learner cannot measure progress yet.

What I would build next

Ordered by what most improves a live demo, following IMPLEMENTATION_ROADMAP.md and MVP_SCOPE.md. Nothing here needs an API key.

  1. Phase C: dashboard, competency profile and gap analysis

    Replace the legacy dashboard with a real home (next action, top gaps, recommended learning) and build /competencies and /competencies/gaps, so the result page's next steps land on finished screens. Adds GET /me/dashboard and GET /me/attempts, both already specified in MVP-19 and MVP-06.

    closes the biggest gap found in B3
  2. Demo pack demo-2: believable synthetic content

    About 3 job roles, 8 competencies and 40 or more scenario-style questions on statistical methods, with explanations, all labelled synthetic. Uses the versioned pack mechanism from phase A.

    closes the biggest gap found in B2
  3. Course catalogue and course detail

    Search and filter by competency, difficulty and duration (DEC-051), plus a detail page showing why a course was recommended and which gap it serves.

    gives recommendations a destination
  4. Lessons, learning player and progress

    Migration proposed in writing first (DEC-049): modules, lessons as replaceable content, ProgressRecord, and a learning path with a "continue learning" card.

    completes the learn → progress loop
  5. Migrate the login page; run a manual NVDA pass

    Login is the last legacy screen in the journey. Record a screen-reader walkthrough of onboarding → result, which automated axe checks cannot replace.

    accessibility sign-off for MVP-F4
  6. Commit, then add CI

    Commit phases A and B on Diw, then run pytest, Vitest, type-check, build and the Playwright journeys on every push.

    protects what is now working
  7. Later: AI/RAG interfaces with local fakes, and the official-data integration plan

    Provider interfaces and fakes only, plus the source-by-source plan for authorisation, licence and citations. Real providers and iGOT wait for your decisions and credentials.

    keeps the platform credible and non-official
